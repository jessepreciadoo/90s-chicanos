---
title: "How did El Congreso organize the hunger strike?"
---

Naomi García, Edwin López, André Vásquez, Salvador Barajas, and Claudia Leiva met at García's Isla Vista apartment and decided to begin a hunger strike. At first, the plan was kept almost secret, partly because students worried about divisions within El Congreso and whether the action would fail. But when press releases announced the strike under the banner "Indigenous Resistance for Social Justice," the broader Chicana/o and Latina/o student community gathered for an emergency meeting at El Centro on April 18, only hours before the strike was supposed to begin. That meeting was intense because some students supported the strike, while others felt it was too sudden or that more organizing should happen first.

After El Congreso agreed to endorse and help plan the hunger strike, students delayed the strike for a week and revised the demands to make them broader and more strategic. They also changed its structure for the strike by creating an executive council and about fifteen subcommittees in charge of outreach, fundraising, security, media, medical services, public relations, research, supplies, and other responsibilities. El Congreso also selected three student negotiators, Benjamin "Benny" Torres, Mike Muñoz, and Marisela Márquez, because the hunger strikers would not be physically able to negotiate while fasting.

This organization shows that the hunger strike was not only about the nine students who fasted. It depended on a much larger network of students, supporters, and behind-the-scenes labor. Their organizing made the hunger strike a collective action rather than an individual sacrifice.
